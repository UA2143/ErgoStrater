DIY Standing Desk Converter Ideas

Want to try working while standing without buying a full standing desk? These simple DIY standing desk converter ideas can help you raise your existing workspace using stable items you may already have.

Beginner 8 min read

Quick answer

A DIY standing desk converter can be made by creating a stable raised platform for your monitor, laptop or keyboard. The simplest approach is to use sturdy books, storage boxes or a strong flat surface that safely raises your equipment.

The most important part is stability. Your DIY setup should not wobble, slide or create a risk of your equipment falling. Start with a small height increase and adjust it based on your working position.

Start here

Why try a standing setup?

A standing desk does not have to replace your normal desk. For many beginners, the goal is simply to have another working position available during the day.

A DIY converter lets you experiment with standing without immediately spending money on a dedicated standing desk.

Think flexible, not permanent:

The best beginner setup is one that lets you switch comfortably between sitting and standing rather than forcing you to stand all day.

DIY idea 01

Use books or storage boxes

If you want to test a standing workspace before buying anything, start with items you already own. Sturdy books and storage boxes can create a temporary raised platform.

  1. Clear a stable area of your desk.
  2. Choose strong books, boxes or another solid platform.
  3. Stack or position them so the surface remains level.
  4. Place your keyboard, mouse or laptop on the raised surface.
  5. Test the setup before adding more height.

Avoid lightweight or unstable objects. If the platform moves when you type, rebuild it rather than simply adding more height.

DIY idea 02

Build a two-level DIY converter

A useful standing desk converter does not always need one large platform. You can create separate levels for your screen and input devices.

For example, your monitor can sit on a higher stable platform while your keyboard and mouse remain on a lower surface.

This approach is especially useful because your screen and hands do not necessarily need to be at exactly the same height.

Keep the setup balanced:

Make sure each platform has enough support for the equipment placed on it. Avoid creating a tall stack that can tip forward or sideways.

Laptop setup

DIY standing setup for a laptop

A laptop is convenient for a temporary standing workstation because the screen and keyboard are already connected.

However, raising the laptop high enough for comfortable viewing also raises the keyboard. This can make long typing sessions less comfortable.

For a short session, a stable raised platform may be enough. For longer sessions, consider pairing the raised laptop with a separate keyboard and mouse.

Find your height

Get the standing desk height right

There is no single DIY platform height that works for everyone. Your chair height, body proportions, desk height and equipment all affect the final position.

Start by standing naturally with your shoulders relaxed. Position your keyboard and mouse so that you can work without constantly reaching upward or shrugging your shoulders.

Then check your screen. You should be able to view it comfortably without repeatedly bending your neck downward.

Make small adjustments:

Add or remove a small amount of height at a time. A stable setup at a slightly imperfect height is better than a tall setup that feels unsafe.

Avoid these

Common DIY standing desk mistakes

  • Using unstable materials: Avoid anything that rocks, bends or slides easily.
  • Making the platform too tall: More height does not automatically mean better ergonomics.
  • Ignoring your keyboard position: Your hands should not have to stay unusually high while typing.
  • Blocking equipment ventilation: Make sure laptops and other devices have adequate airflow.
  • Trying to stand continuously: A standing option is meant to add flexibility, not force one working position.

Budget principle

You do not need to buy a standing desk first

DIY experimentation can help you understand what you actually need before spending money on a dedicated standing desk or converter.

Start with what you already have. Test the height, stability and comfort. If you use the setup regularly, you can then decide whether a purpose-built converter is worth the upgrade.

This is the ErgoStarter approach: use what you already have, fix the position, then upgrade where it matters.
