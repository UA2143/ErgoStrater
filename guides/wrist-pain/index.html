How to Stop Wrist Pain While Typing on a Laptop

Long laptop sessions can leave your wrists feeling uncomfortable, especially when your keyboard is too high, too low or positioned at an awkward angle. These simple setup changes can help reduce unnecessary wrist strain without expensive equipment.

Beginner 7 min read

Quick answer

To reduce wrist strain while typing on a laptop, keep your keyboard at a comfortable height, avoid prolonged extreme wrist angles, and keep your shoulders relaxed while typing.

If raising your laptop screen improves your viewing position but makes the keyboard too high, consider using a separate keyboard and mouse for longer work sessions.

Step 1

Check your current setup

Before buying anything, look at how you naturally position your hands while typing. Your keyboard position, desk height and chair height all affect how your wrists feel during a long session.

Sit in your normal working position and notice whether your shoulders are relaxed and whether your wrists are being forced into an awkward angle.

Start with the obvious:

If you are constantly reaching upward, bending your wrists sharply or resting your body weight on your wrists while typing, your setup may need adjustment.

Keyboard setup

Fix your keyboard height

A keyboard that is too high can encourage you to lift your shoulders or bend your wrists. A keyboard that is too low can also feel awkward depending on your chair and desk height.

Try to position the keyboard so you can type without deliberately bending your wrists upward or keeping your shoulders tense.

  1. Sit normally in your chair.
  2. Relax your shoulders.
  3. Place your hands over the keyboard.
  4. Notice whether your wrists feel forced into an uncomfortable angle.
  5. Adjust your chair, desk or keyboard position gradually.

Wrist position

Find a more comfortable wrist position

Your wrists do not need to be held rigidly in one exact position. The main goal is to avoid spending long periods in a position that feels strained or uncomfortable.

Keep your typing area close enough that you are not constantly reaching forward. Let your fingers move naturally across the keyboard instead of putting unnecessary pressure through your wrists.

Do not force it:

Ergonomic setup is about reducing unnecessary strain, not holding your hands perfectly still. If a position feels uncomfortable, make a small adjustment and reassess.

Mouse setup

Check your mouse position

Wrist discomfort is not always caused by the keyboard. A mouse positioned too far away can make you repeatedly reach forward or sideways.

Keep the mouse close enough that you can use it without stretching your arm or lifting your shoulder.

  • Keep the mouse close to the keyboard.
  • Avoid repeatedly reaching across a large desk.
  • Relax your shoulder while using the mouse.
  • Move your whole hand and arm naturally rather than gripping the mouse tightly.

Work habits

Take regular movement breaks

Even a well-arranged desk can become uncomfortable if you stay in the same position for too long. Changing position regularly gives your hands, wrists, shoulders and back a chance to move.

During longer work sessions, briefly step away from the keyboard, move your hands and change your position rather than remaining completely static.

Simple habit:

Use natural breaks in your workflow as reminders to stand up, move around and reset your working position.

Laptop problem

What to do when your laptop is raised

Raising a laptop screen can improve the viewing position, but it also raises the built-in keyboard. This creates a common trade-off for laptop users.

If the laptop is raised significantly and you are typing for long periods, a separate keyboard and mouse can allow you to keep the screen higher while keeping your hands lower on the desk.

Avoid these

Common wrist setup mistakes

  • Typing with constantly raised shoulders: Recheck your chair, desk and keyboard height.
  • Keeping the mouse too far away: Move it closer so you do not repeatedly reach.
  • Resting heavily on your wrists: Avoid putting unnecessary pressure on them while typing.
  • Ignoring discomfort: If a setup repeatedly causes pain or discomfort, change position rather than simply pushing through it.
  • Changing everything at once: Make one or two small adjustments so you can understand what actually improves your setup.

Budget approach

Fix your setup before buying new gear

Wrist comfort does not always require an expensive keyboard, mouse or desk. Start by checking your existing chair, desk height, keyboard position and mouse placement.

If you still have a problem after adjusting your setup, then consider whether a specific piece of equipment would solve that problem.
